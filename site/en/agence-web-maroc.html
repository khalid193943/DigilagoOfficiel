What does a web agency in Morocco actually do?

A web agency in Morocco designs, develops, launches and keeps your website alive: design, code, copy, SEO, hosting and maintenance, with one team and a single point of contact. Choosing an agency therefore means choosing a partner for years to come, not just a supplier.

Web agency, digital agency, web development agency: in Morocco, providers go by different names depending on their specialty:

  • Web agency: showcase websites, custom websites and online stores.
  • Digital agency: a broader scope, often adding social media, online advertising and brand identity.
  • Web development agency: focused on the technical side, for web and mobile apps, platforms and business software.
  • SEO agency: specialized in ranking on Google, sometimes without building websites.

For an SME, the ideal is an agency that brings together the website, the Google Business Profile, SEO and ongoing support: you avoid coordinating three providers who keep passing the buck. To understand the steps of a project, read our guide to website design in Morocco.

Web agency, freelancer or DIY website: what’s the difference?

An agency brings a full team and continuity; a freelancer, direct contact and often a smaller budget; a DIY website, a starting point if you have the time. The more customers your website needs to bring in, the more expert support matters.

How do you spot the best web agency in Morocco for your project?

There’s no such thing as the “best web agency in Morocco” in absolute terms: the best one is the one that understands your business, shows verifiable results, gives you a clear price in writing and stays reachable after launch. Five criteria help you spot it.

  1. Verifiable work. Live websites you can open on your phone, ideally in your industry.
  2. Fast, well-ranked websites. Search Google for the trade and city of one of its clients: does their website show up?
  3. Listening. A good agency asks about your customers and your goals before talking tech.
  4. Transparency. A detailed written quote, no hidden fees, and clear answers about who owns the website.
  5. Availability. A named point of contact, reachable on WhatsApp or by phone, who replies quickly.

The “top 10 agencies” rankings you find online are sometimes sponsored: rely instead on the websites the agency has actually built and on its Google reviews.

What questions should you ask a web agency before signing?

Ask specific questions about price, timelines, ownership, SEO and ongoing support: a serious agency answers them plainly, and in writing. Here are the ten questions to keep handy.

  1. Will the website be custom-designed or built from a theme?
  2. What’s included in the price, and what isn’t?
  3. Can I see a first version before paying in full?
  4. How many rounds of revisions are included?
  5. Will the domain name, hosting and code be in my name?
  6. Who writes the copy in French, Arabic and English?
  7. What exactly do you do for SEO and the Google Business Profile?
  8. What are the annual costs after launch?
  9. Who answers me if there’s a problem, through which channel and how quickly?
  10. Will I be able to edit my content myself, and will I be trained?

What are the red flags when choosing a web agency?

Be wary of an agency that makes promises without putting them in writing, keeps your access details or guarantees the top spot on Google: these are the three most common red flags. Others are just as telling.

  • “Guaranteed top spot on Google.” No one can guarantee it: Google alone decides its rankings. See our guide to SEO in Morocco.
  • No written quote, or a one-line quote along the lines of “website: X MAD”.
  • A domain name registered in the agency’s name, or a refusal to hand over your access details.
  • A portfolio you can’t verify: screenshots, but no live website to visit.
  • Websites that are slow on mobile among the work on show.
  • An abnormally low price with no explanation: generic theme, no copy, no follow-up.
  • Not a word about maintenance or annual costs.
  • An agency you can’t reach even before signing: it will only get worse afterward.

What should a good website design quote include?

A good quote spells out exactly what you get, at what price, on what timeline and under what terms, so there’s no room for unpleasant surprises later. If it fits on one line, ask for the details.

  • Scope: number of pages, languages, features (form, WhatsApp, booking, CMI card payment or cash on delivery).
  • Content: who writes the copy, who supplies the photos, who handles translations.
  • SEO: what’s done at launch (titles, structured data, Search Console, Google Business Profile).
  • Timeline: first version date, number of revisions, launch date.
  • Recurring costs: domain name, hosting and maintenance, every year.
  • Ownership: domain, code, content and access in your name.
  • Payment: payment schedule, amounts excluding or including VAT, a proper invoice.
  • Training and support after launch.

To put a quote in perspective, keep in mind the indicative ranges seen in Morocco: roughly 3,000 to 8,000 MAD for a showcase website built from a theme, 8,000 to 25,000 MAD for a custom, multilingual showcase website, and 15,000 to 60,000 MAD for an online store. Full details are in our guide to website prices in Morocco.

Who owns the website, the domain name and the code?

They should belong to you: the domain name is registered in your name, and both the content and the code are transferred to you, in black and white in the quote or contract. That’s your protection if you ever change providers.

  • Domain name: you are the registered owner, with your own login at the registrar.
  • Code and files: you can get a copy at any time.
  • Content: the copy and photos produced for you belong to you; check the rights to any images used.
  • Google accounts: your Google Business Profile, Search Console and Analytics are created in your name; the agency is simply invited.

Example: a private school in El Jadida that switches agencies should be able to take back its domain and website with no negotiation and no surprise fees. If that’s not possible, the contract was poorly drafted.

Do you need a web agency in Casablanca, Rabat or Marrakech to get good service?

No: an agency can perfectly well support a business in another city, since most of the work is done remotely. What matters isn’t distance, but responsiveness, clear communication and knowledge of your local market.

Searching for a “web agency Casablanca,” a “web agency Rabat” or a “web agency Marrakech” is a natural reflex, but looking across all of Morocco widens your options. A web agency in El Jadida can build the website of a dentist in Casablanca, a riad in Fez or a caftan boutique in Marrakech just as well as an agency next door, as long as it takes an interest in their customers and the words those customers type into Google. A well-run remote project goes like this:

  1. A short first conversation, by phone, video call or WhatsApp.
  2. Sending your details: logo, photos, copy, opening hours, services.
  3. A link to a first version, to open on your phone.
  4. Your feedback, by text or voice message, followed by the adjustments.
  5. Launch, the Google Business Profile and a short training session by video call.
  6. Regular follow-up, with an in-person meeting when it helps.

Questions

In short,the answers.

How much does a web agency cost in Morocco?

The price depends on the project more than on the agency: as a rough guide, expect about 3,000 to 8,000 MAD for a showcase website built from a theme, 8,000 to 25,000 MAD for a custom, multilingual showcase website, and 15,000 to 60,000 MAD for an online store. These ranges vary from one provider to another. Insist on a written quote that also details the annual costs.

What is the best web agency in Morocco?

There’s no best agency in absolute terms, only the one best suited to your project. Check its live work, how fast its websites are on a phone, how visible they are on Google and what its clients say in their reviews. A serious agency states its price in writing and registers the domain name in your name.

Web agency or freelancer: which should you choose?

A freelancer is a good fit for a simple, well-defined project, often on a smaller budget. An agency brings a full team (design, development, content, SEO) and continuity if someone is away. The more your website needs to bring in customers over the long term, the more an agency makes sense.

Should you choose a web agency in your own city?

It isn’t necessary: an agency can support a business in Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech or Tangier entirely remotely, by phone, video call and WhatsApp. What matters is its responsiveness and its understanding of your local market. An in-person meeting is still possible when it helps.

Should the domain name be in my name?

Yes, always. Whether it’s a .ma or a .com, the domain name must be registered with you as the owner, and you must have your own login details. Otherwise, you risk losing your web address the day you change providers.

Should you pay for the whole website before work starts?

It’s not advisable. A common practice is to pay a deposit at the start, then the balance on delivery, once you’ve approved the work. Some agencies, like Digilago, even show you a first version before any commitment.

What should a website quote include?

A serious quote specifies the number of pages and languages, the features, who provides the copy and photos, what’s done for SEO, the timeline, the annual costs and who owns the website. It also sets out the payment terms and whether amounts exclude or include VAT. A one-line quote makes it impossible to compare offers.

Can an agency guarantee the top spot on Google?

No. No one controls Google’s rankings, and an agency that guarantees the top spot should make you suspicious. A sound method (fast website, useful content, complete Google Business Profile, reviews) moves you closer step by step; for competitive searches, generally expect three to six months of steady work.

How do you switch web agencies without losing your website?

First, get back your access: domain name, hosting, code, Google Business Profile, Search Console and Analytics. Make a full backup of the website and its content before any migration. If your contract states that everything is in your name, the switch is painless.
